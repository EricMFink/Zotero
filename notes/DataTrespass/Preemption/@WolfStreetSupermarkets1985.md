 "At some point after the picketing commenced, plaintiff filed an unfair labor practice charge with the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) alleging unlawful recognitional picketing." [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/RN8WAHV9?page=3&annotation=highlight-p3x50y617)




 "we need not decide whether the defendants here have constitutional immunity from liability for tortious interference with business relations arising out of peaceful picketing, since plaintiff claims damages for recognitional picketing and State tort law in this area is largely preempted by Federal labor la Plaintiff insists that the defendants were engaged in unlawful recognitional picketing in violation of National Labor Relations Act § 8 (b) (7) (C)" [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/RN8WAHV9?page=3&annotation=highlight-p3x311y177)




 "Defendants' conduct was arguably protected or prohibited under the NLRB so we begin with the presumption that State tort law is preempted and the matter is one within the exclusive jurisdiction of the NLRB" [Page 4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/RN8WAHV9?page=4&annotation=highlight-p4x50y350)




 "the presumption may be overcome and the States are not required to yield jurisdiction when the conduct touches concerns deeply rooted in local feelings or responsibilities, or the proposed State regulation of that conduct would implicate the concerns underlying the Federal labor laws only peripherally" [Page 4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/RN8WAHV9?page=4&annotation=highlight-p4x50y234)
