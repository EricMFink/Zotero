### In-text annotations

 "In 2011, the union began organizing and conducting demonstrations at Walmart stores across the United States, including in California. The demonstrations were part of a union campaign designed to induce Walmart to provide its employees with better working conditions and pay. The campaign also sought to pressure Walmart to reinstate employees the union alleged Walmart had discharged or disciplined for exercising their rights under the NLRA. The union publicly indicated it was not seeking to act as the representative or bargaining agent for Walmart employees" [Page 1](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/C77BE2WC?page=1&annotation=highlight-p1x79y77)




 "In March 2013, Walmart filed an unfair labor practice charge with the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB). In the charge, Walmart alleged defendants violated section 8(b)(1)(A) of the NLRA (Section 8(b)(1)(A); 29 U.S.C. § 158(b)(1)(A)) by "planning, orchestrating, and conducting a series of unauthorized and blatantly trespassory in-store mass demonstrations, invasive `flash mobs,' and other confrontational group activities at numerous facilities nationwide ... by which the UFCW restrained and coerced employees in the exercise of their Section 7 rights (which includes the right to refrain from supporting the UFCW) by attempting to impose its will on local facility management in front of facility employees through the sheer force of a mass of moving bodies despite requests and direction by local management to leave."" [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/C77BE2WC?page=2&annotation=highlight-p2x79y446)




 "The record does not indicate what action, if any, the NLRB took on Walmart's unfair labor practice charge. However, in June 2013, Walmart's counsel represented in an Arkansas court that Walmart had withdrawn the charges with respect to the in-store demonstrations" [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/C77BE2WC?page=2&annotation=highlight-p2x79y308)




 "In May 2013, Walmart filed a complaint in the Los Angeles superior court for trespass, seeking injunctive and declaratory relief against the union" [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/C77BE2WC?page=2&annotation=highlight-p2x79y265)




 "The union argued the action was preempted because the NLRA arguably prohibited the union conduct Walmart was seeking to enjoin." [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/C77BE2WC?page=2&annotation=highlight-p2x79y49)




 "The union did not argue its conduct was arguably protected under the" [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/C77BE2WC?page=2&annotation=highlight-p2x227y49)




 "NLRA" [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/C77BE2WC?page=3&annotation=highlight-p3x80y744)




 "if the conduct at issue is arguably prohibited or protected otherwise applicable state law and procedures are ordinarily pre-empted. [Citation.] When, however, the conduct at issue is only a peripheral concern of the Act or touches on interests so deeply rooted in local feeling and responsibility" [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/C77BE2WC?page=3&annotation=highlight-p3x80y47)




 "that, in the absence of compelling congressional direction, it could not be inferred that Congress intended to deprive the State of the power to act, we refuse to invalidate state regulation or sanction of the conduct." (Operating Engineers v. Jones (1983) 460 U.S. 669, 676 [75 L.Ed.2d 368, 103 S.Ct. 1453] (Jones);" [Page 4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/C77BE2WC?page=4&annotation=highlight-p4x79y694)




 "If, despite being arguably protected or prohibited by the NLRA, the conduct at issue involves a deeply rooted local interest, the question of whether the state action is allowed "involves a sensitive balancing of any harm to the regulatory scheme established by Congress, either in terms of negating the Board's exclusive jurisdiction or in terms of conflicting substantive rules, and the importance of the asserted cause of action to the State as a protection to its citizens." (Jones, at p. 676.)" [Page 4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/C77BE2WC?page=4&annotation=highlight-p4x79y511)




 "The union does not contend the challenged conduct is arguably protected under the NLRA.[7] Instead, the union asserts the NLRA "arguably prohibits" *202 the union's challenged conduct, triggering preemption. Although Walmart appears to assert some portions of the union's conduct fall outside of Garmon preemption rules, it too argues at least some of the union's conduct is arguably prohibited under the NLRA. But that the NLRA arguably prohibits the challenged conduct does not end our analysis.[8] 202 Under Garmon guidelines, state action concerning arguably prohibited conduct is "ordinarily preempted." (Jones, supra, 460 U.S. at p. 676.) In other words, there is a presumption of preemption. (Belknap, Inc. v. Hale (1983) 463 U.S. 491, 498 [77 L.Ed.2d 798, 103 S.Ct. 3172].) Yet, as noted above, when the "conduct at issue is only a peripheral concern of the Act," or "touches on interests so deeply rooted in local feeling and responsibility" that we cannot infer that Congress intended to deprive the state of the ability to regulate or sanction the conduct, the presumption of preemption is overcome. (Jones, supra, 460 U.S. at p. 676.) We conclude the local interest exception applies" [Page 4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/C77BE2WC?page=4&annotation=highlight-p4x52y214)
