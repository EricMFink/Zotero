 "In Animal Legal Defense Fund v. Reynolds, 8 F.4th 781" [Page 2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=2&annotation=highlight-p2x310y52)




 "we ruled that the prohibition on making false statements in an employment application was insufficiently tailored [**3] and unconstitutional, because it encompassed statements that were not material to an employment decision 8th Cir. 2021) ("ALDF I"), this court considered an Iowa law that prohibited (1) accessing an agricultural production facility by false pretenses and (2) making a false statement or misrepresentation as part of an application for employment at such a facility. We concluded that the prohibition on accessing a facility by false pretenses did not violate the free speech clause of the First Amendment. But" [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=3&annotation=highlight-p3x47y577)




 "A new Iowa law solves the materiality problem in the employment provision by forbidding the use of deception "on a matter that would reasonably result in a denial of an opportunity to be employed." The statute also narrows the scope of both prohibitions by adding an intent element: the law forbids the use of deceptive speech only when the person gains access or employment "with the intent to cause physical or economic harm or other injury" to the agricultural production facility." [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=3&annotation=highlight-p3x47y435)




 "The statute at issue in ALDF I concerned the offense of "agricultural production facility fraud."" [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=3&annotation=highlight-p3x47y315)




 "While the appeal in ALDF I was pending, the Iowa General Assembly passed a new bill entitled "Agricultural Production Facility Trespass."" [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=3&annotation=highlight-p3x309y440)




 "A person commits agricultural production facility trespass if the person does any of the following: a. Uses deception . . . on a matter that would reasonably result in a denial of access to an agricultural production facility that is not open to the public, and, through such deception, gains access to the agricultural production facility, with the intent to cause physical or economic harm or other injury to the agricultural production facility's operations, agricultural animals, crop, owner, personnel, equipment, building, premises, business interest, or customer. b. Uses deception . . . on a matter that would reasonably result in a denial of an opportunity to be employed at an agricultural production facility that is not open to the public, and, through such deception, is so employed, with the intent to cause physical or economic harm or other injury to the agricultural production facility's operations, agricultural animals, crop, owner, personnel, equipment, building, premises, business [**6] interest, or customer." [Page 3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=3&annotation=highlight-p3x329y131)




 "The Employment Provision proscribes gaining employment through false speech on matters that are material to hiring. where false claims are made to secure offers of employment, "it is well established that the Government may restrict speech without affronting the First Amendment." 567 U.S. at 723 (plurality opinion)" [Page 4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=4&annotation=highlight-p4x47y288)




 "the new Iowa statute adds an intent requirement that narrows the statute so that it proscribes fewer false utterances" [Page 4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=4&annotation=highlight-p4x47y237)




 "A person is guilty of agricultural production facility trespass only if he gains access or employment "with the intent to cause physical or economic harm or other injury to the agricultural production facility's operations, agricultural animals, crop, owner, personnel, equipment, building, premises, business interest, or customer."" [Page 4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=4&annotation=highlight-p4x47y159)




 "The disputed issue is whether this intent requirement grafts an impermissible viewpoint-based restriction onto regulations that otherwise conform to the First Amendment." [Page 4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=4&annotation=highlight-p4x47y94)




 "The Iowa statute is likewise consistent with the First Amendment. Each provision permissibly forbids false statements that result in a legally cognizable harm." [Page 5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=5&annotation=highlight-p5x47y643)




 "As in Dinwiddie, the supplemental intent requirement does not distinguish [*1070] among speakers based on their viewpoints. If a person uses deception to gain employment with intent to harm a facility, the offender would be liable for deceptively praising the facility ("I love the work you do, and I want to support it!") or deceptively criticizing the facility ("This facility is poorly managed, and I will help increase profits.")." [Page 5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=5&annotation=highlight-p5x47y474)




 "The intent element determines whether particular conduct violates the statute, but it does not mean that a violation turns on the viewpoint of an offender's deceptive speech. the intent requirement permissibly reflects "the general view that criminal punishment should be reserved for those who intend the harm they commit." Kelly, 9 F.4th at 1253 (Hartz, J., dissenting). The statute filters out trespassers who are relatively innocuous, and focuses the criminal law on conduct that inflicts greater harm on victims and society." [Page 5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=5&annotation=highlight-p5x309y294)






The intent requirement doesn't necessarily preclude liability for employee activity that is protected under the NLRA. For example, workplace recording to document and expose unfair labor practices or unsafe conditions, or salting to build support for a union organizing campaign, might be regarded as harmful to the employer's economic or property interests. While state courts might avoid the problem by interpreting the statute to require an intent to inflict such harm, they might interpret it more brutally as merely requiring an intent to commit an act likely to cause such harm. Even if employee defendants ultimately prevail on ground that they didn't specifically intend to harm employers' economic interests, the potential for a lawsuit would have a chilling effect on PCA. Preemption reduces that risk.  [Page 5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/MA7VE3DP?page=5&annotation=text-p5x304y114)
